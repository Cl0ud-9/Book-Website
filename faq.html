Reading

How do I read a book here?

Open any book and press Read now. It opens right in your browser: on a laptop you turn the pages with the arrows, the arrow keys or a swipe, and on a phone you simply scroll.

Will it remember where I stopped?

Yes. Open the book again and it picks up on your page, and the home page lists the books you are reading. It is kept on this device.

Can I change how the text looks?

Press Text in the reader: make the type bigger or smaller, choose a Paper, Sepia or Night page, and read in Literata (a serif made for reading on screens), Georgia or a sans.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes, for the books you keep on this device: press Keep for offline reading on a book's page, or in the reader's Text settings, and it opens with no connection at all. Downloaded books work anywhere too.

Downloads

Can I keep a book?

Yes. Every book page has an EPUB download, the format almost every reading app uses.

Can I read on a Kindle or a Kobo?

Yes. Each book page links to a Kindle edition and a Kobo edition. You can also send the EPUB to a Kindle with Amazon's Send to Kindle.

What app opens an EPUB on my phone?

Apple Books on an iPhone, and Google Play Books or any EPUB reader on Android. Or skip the app and press Read now.

The books

Where do the books come from?

Almost all are Standard Ebooks editions: public-domain classics carefully proofread and typeset by volunteers, with covers made from public-domain paintings. A few classics from India that Standard Ebooks does not have come from Project Gutenberg. Each book's page says which edition it is.

How are the books ranked?

By how much they are read. The order comes from Standard Ebooks' popularity ranking, so All books starts with the most read, and Where to start on the home page is the top ten.

How is the book of the month chosen?

A new one on the first of every month, chosen for how much it is read, here and on Open Library, how readers rate it, and the time of year: ghost stories in October, A Christmas Carol in December. No book comes round twice in a year, nor an author twice in six months, and every third month is a short read. The home page says why each one was chosen.

Will there be more books?

Yes, a few at a time. Every new one gets its own page, with a cover, a description and the download.

Free and fair

Are the books really free?

Yes. They are in the public domain, so they belong to everyone. No ads, no catch, and no account needed.

Why are there no new bestsellers or manga?

Recent books, such as Colleen Hoover's, and manga such as One Piece are still under copyright, so they cannot be shared for free. Your local library can lend you most of them, often as ebooks through an app like Libby.

Are they free where I live?

Every book here is in the public domain in the United States and in India, where copyright lasts for the author's life and sixty years. That goes for translators, illustrators and cover painters too: where a cover painting is still under copyright in India, the book has a cover drawn here instead. Most books are free almost everywhere, but check your country's rules.

Do you track what I read?

We count, without cookies and without knowing who you are, how often each page is visited and how often each book is opened, reaches its second chapter, is finished or is downloaded. That is how the book of the month learns what people enjoy. If your browser asks sites not to track you, we count nothing at all. Your reading stays on your own device.

What do you keep about me?

The privacy page says exactly what is kept, where, and how to remove it. Read it

Your account

Do I need an account?

No. Reading, downloading, your place in each book, your highlights, notes and reading time all work without one. They are kept on your own device, and Your reading shows them all.

Can I move my notes to another phone or laptop?

Yes. On Your reading, Back up saves everything to one small file; open Your reading on the other device and choose Restore with that file.
